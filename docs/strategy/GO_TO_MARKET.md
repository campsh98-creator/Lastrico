# Motia and Lastrico go-to-market strategy

**Status:** validation plan, not a traction claim  
**Initial market:** Milan and nearby validated coverage  
**Principle:** *Build in public, solve a visible problem, let the community improve the
product, and convert trust into adoption.*

## Strategic wedge

Lastrico enters through a specific job:

> Compare a normal route with a credible alternative that reduces exposure to known
> uncomfortable road surfaces without imposing an unreasonable detour.

This is narrow enough to test in Milan and broad enough to reveal whether people value
transparent route trade-offs. Motia should not launch as a generic “AI Google Maps
competitor”. The platform story follows evidence from the wedge.

## Priority segments

| Segment | Pain and current workaround | Initial value proposition | Primary channel | Activation event | Retention hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Milan motorcyclists and scooter riders | Rough surfaces affect comfort and control; rely on memory or generic routing | Compare estimated surface exposure with a bounded time trade-off | Clubs, forums, local creators, shops, university networks | Completes one route and understands the trade-off | Repeated commuting and trusted local corrections |
| Surface-sensitive drivers | Low-profile tyres, rigid suspension, classic cars, passengers; manually avoid known streets | A smoother known alternative with explicit delay and unknown coverage | Automotive groups, local search, garages, owner clubs | Selects a lower-exposure alternative | Saved preference and repeat corridor |
| Cyclists | Surface and legal access matter differently; generic car substitutions are unacceptable | Mode-specific routing and honest surface evidence | Cycling communities, civic tech, universities | Completes a legal bicycle route | Local knowledge, corrections, recurring routes |
| Professional/urban fleet design partners | Driver comfort, reliability, vehicle suitability, and route consistency affect operations | Route-condition insight and a measured pilot—not immediate fleet replacement | Direct founder outreach, fleet associations, partners | Agrees pilot KPI and shares structured feedback | Operational dashboard and repeat route evidence |
| Municipal and road-data partners | Fragmented observations and weak public feedback loops | Structured, provenance-aware condition evidence | Direct institutional outreach and innovation programmes | Supplies or reviews one dataset/pilot | Coverage report and correction workflow |

Secondary audiences—investors, developers, researchers, advisors, and media—support capital,
credibility, and contribution. They are not substitutes for active users.

## Positioning messages

### User

“Choose the route trade-off that fits your journey. Lastrico compares known road-surface
conditions and shows what remains unknown.”

### Contributor

“Help improve the evidence: test a route, correct a road segment, document a bug, or improve
the open-source product.”

### Partner

“Pilot an explainable road-intelligence layer with measured coverage, provenance, and route
outcomes.”

### Investor

“Lastrico is the working wedge. Motia is the conditional platform opportunity. Capital funds
the evidence required to connect them.”

Avoid “safe route”, accident-prevention, complete Milan coverage, production AI, national
coverage, or automotive availability claims.

## Launch sequence

### 1. Evidence cohort

- Recruit 30–60 deliberately varied testers across car, motorcycle, and bicycle modes.
- Use predefined Milan routes and nearby boundary cases.
- Conduct observed onboarding and post-route interviews.
- Publish latency, route completion, unknown coverage, and corrected defects.
- Stop expansion until route-selection and GPS failures are within agreed thresholds.

### 2. Local controlled beta

- Invite waitlisted users only where coverage is explicitly supported.
- Publish a coverage map and data date.
- Introduce post-trip reporting and contributor status.
- Partner with two or three credible local communities.
- Test referral language based on usefulness, not incentives alone.

### 3. Regional proof

- Add nearby municipalities through a versioned release.
- Test whether the same taxonomy works outside central Milan.
- Run one paid or co-funded fleet/municipal pilot with pre-agreed outcomes.
- Test one consumer premium hypothesis without locking core safety information behind payment.

### 4. Contrasting-city and corridor pilots

- Select Rome or Catania only after data and moderation readiness.
- Run a long-distance north–south corridor benchmark.
- Demonstrate that Motia's model generalises beyond cobblestones without claiming nationwide
  hazard coverage.

## Product-led and community mechanics

- Route comparison cards show the reason and trade-off.
- Every route can produce a shareable, privacy-safe summary.
- Post-trip feedback asks whether the chosen trade-off was worthwhile.
- Nontechnical reporting uses a small category set, automatic map matching, and later review.
- Contributors see report status, correction outcome, and recognition.
- GitHub includes beginner issues, public decisions, contribution guides, and release notes.
- High-value contributors may enter testing or advisory groups, but accountable roles remain
  professionally owned.

## Founder-led distribution

The founder should document:

- why a specific route failed or improved;
- current beta constraints;
- field tests;
- how road evidence is verified;
- provider and data trade-offs;
- what community feedback changed;
- business-plan assumptions and corrections.

Founder content needs a sustainable cadence and must not overstate technical authority.
Founder biography remains subject to verification before external publication.

## Low-budget channel plan

### Highest priority

- Local and problem-led SEO.
- YouTube demonstrations and founder build logs.
- Instagram/TikTok excerpts from real tests.
- GitHub and civic-tech communities.
- Motorcycle, cycling, automotive, and university communities.
- Earned local and specialist media.
- Direct design-partner outreach.
- Email update for testers, contributors, and investor leads with separate consent.

### Selective paid amplification

Use small tests only after a message or channel produces retained users organically. Paid
media amplifies a proven demonstration; it does not manufacture product-market fit.

## First experiments

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Primary success metric | Stop/learn rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Milan route-comparison landing page | A visible trade-off drives qualified beta interest | Eligible sign-up rate | Stop if sign-ups do not match supported geography/modes |
| Motorcycle community field test | Surface-aware comparison solves a repeated problem | Second-route rate | Rework if users value data but not navigation |
| Founder route teardown video | Transparent failures build qualified trust | Demo visits and watch completion | Stop vanity-format production without product actions |
| Post-trip correction | A two-minute flow yields usable evidence | Verified report rate | Simplify if abandonment or invalid submissions dominate |
| Fleet design-partner interview | Route consistency and condition evidence matter commercially | Paid pilot proposal | Stop custom enterprise build before a paid problem is clear |
| Premium preference test | A saved advanced preference has willingness to pay | Paid-intent conversion | Do not infer demand from survey answers alone |

## Metrics

### Product

- first successful route;
- preview-to-navigation conversion;
- completed sessions;
- non-fastest alternative selection;
- route regret and trade-off understanding;
- D30/D90 retention;
- crash-free sessions, latency, and reroute reliability.

### Data and community

- recent verified kilometres;
- unknown distance per route;
- valid, confirmed, rejected, and expired reports;
- moderation turnaround;
- contributor second-action rate;
- accepted code/documentation contributions.

### Commercial

- qualified pilot pipeline;
- paid pilot conversion;
- B2C paid intent and conversion;
- revenue and provider cost per session;
- acquisition cost by retained user;
- referral-to-retained-user rate.

Follower count, impressions, and GitHub stars are diagnostic—not proof of product-market fit.

## Geographic expansion gate

A zone opens only when:

- routing and address search are production-capable;
- mode-specific field tests pass;
- evidence completeness and unknown distance are published;
- data licences are approved;
- moderation capacity exists;
- support and rollback ownership are named;
- users cannot confuse map availability with verified hazard coverage.

## Commercial guardrails

- No paid tier may imply guaranteed safety.
- Identifiable movement histories are not sold.
- Data/API products respect ODbL, source licences, consent, and redistribution rights.
- A B2B feature does not silently degrade the consumer route.
- Enterprise customisation cannot override legal access.
- Crowdfunding leads remain separate from product consent and marketing lists.
