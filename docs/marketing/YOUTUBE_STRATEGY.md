# YouTube strategy

## Role

YouTube is the long-form evidence library and the main source for low-cost repurposing. The
goal is qualified understanding and product action, not subscriber count alone.

## Formats

| Format | Length | Example | Evidence requirement |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| founder field note | 6–10 min | Why this routing problem led to Lastrico | current product screen and explicit limits |
| product test | 8–15 min | Fastest versus lower rough-surface exposure | method, date, area, mode, provider |
| education | 6–12 min | Why route ranking is more than one number | sources and current/planned labels |
| open-source session | 10–20 min | Reproducing a routing bug | repository version and no secrets |
| interview | 15–30 min | A rider, researcher, or contributor perspective | consent and no implied endorsement |
| Short | 20–60 sec | one result from a full test | link to complete context |

Initial working titles may include:

- “Why a navigation app can choose an uncomfortable road to save four minutes.”
- “How Lastrico compares known road-surface exposure.”
- “What open source can—and cannot—solve in mobility.”
- “From Lastrico to Motia: the future interface of navigation is the prompt.”

Avoid using another map brand in a title as an accusation. A comparison must be
reproducible, fair, and compliant with relevant terms and trademark rules.

## Production workflow

1. Approve a one-page evidence brief.
2. Capture product screens and road footage safely.
3. Record a concise founder narrative; do not script invented technical authority.
4. Add captions, transcript, chapters, data/provider attribution, beta status, and safety
   limitations.
5. Publish the long-form asset first.
6. Derive up to five Shorts and channel-specific assets.
7. Measure watch time, qualified clicks, first routes, joins, and contributions at 7/30 days.

## First eight episodes

1. The problem and current Milan experiment.
2. A route-comparison test and its limitations.
3. How surface data can be incomplete.
4. Car, motorcycle, and bicycle profiles are not interchangeable.
5. A bug report from discovery to verified fix.
6. The cost and constraints of public map services.
7. How a non-technical person can contribute responsibly.
8. Prompted navigation as a planned, bounded preference interface.

## Collaboration criteria

Invite a creator or expert only when the topic, audience, evidence, and disclosure are clear.
Provide demo access but no prescribed positive conclusion. Aspirational creators such as
Marcello Ascani are examples, not contacts, supporters, or partners.

## Paid promotion gate

Test paid YouTube distribution only after one organic video demonstrates above-baseline
qualified actions, the landing page and activation events work, the relevant region is
supported, and a capped budget/stop condition is approved. Initial tests amplify the best
evidence asset; they do not fund generic brand awareness. Stop if activated-user cost
exceeds the experiment cap or retention is materially below the organic cohort.

## Safety and rights

No handheld filming while driving or riding. Use a passenger, preconfigured fixed equipment,
controlled off-road capture where appropriate, or post-journey playback. Clear music,
footage, map, participant, and location rights before publication.
