# Motia investor FAQ

**Status:** investor working draft  
**Evidence cut-off:** 30 July 2026  
**Not an offer, financial advice, legal opinion, or guarantee**

## Company and product

### What is Motia?

Motia is the proposed parent mobility-intelligence company. Its planned platform is Motia
Routing Intelligence and its conditional future engine/API is Motia Routing Engine. Company
formation, ownership, registered name, and trademark status require verification.

### What is Lastrico?

Lastrico by Motia is the first product and validation wedge. It is currently an experimental
open-source PWA tested in Milan that compares car, motorcycle, and bicycle routes using
known road-surface evidence.

### What works today?

Repository evidence supports MapLibre mapping, public Nominatim search, public
Valhalla/OSRM routing, distinct travel modes, estimated rough-surface exposure, foreground
GPS/manoeuvre experiments, and community report verification states.

It does not establish a production SLA, repeat use, willingness to pay, national data,
native mobile navigation, operating moderation, CarPlay/Android Auto availability, or
safety outcomes.

### Is Motia replacing Google Maps or Waze?

That is not the initial strategy. Motia intends to use or interoperate with a contracted
navigation foundation while owning a differentiated preference, road-evidence, ranking, and
explanation layer. Direct competition or deeper self-hosting is conditional on evidence and
economics.

### Why start with cobblestones?

They create a visible and measurable trade-off: some users accept a bounded delay to reduce
known rough-surface exposure. Milan is the first test area, not the final brand boundary.

## Technology and data

### What does “the future interface of navigation is the prompt” mean?

A user describes priorities in natural language. A constrained interpreter converts them
into a versioned allow-listed schema that the user confirms. Deterministic graph routing,
legal constraints, provider validation, and scoring still decide which legal candidates
exist.

### Does Motia have a proprietary AI model?

No proprietary foundation model is evidenced. The planned differentiation is the combined
preference ontology, data governance, geospatial processing, route scoring, feedback,
moderation, integrations, and user experience.

### Can a prompt request an illegal route?

It can contain any text, but the system must reject or ignore unsupported and illegal
constraints. Traffic law, road access, and mode-specific restrictions cannot be overridden.

### How does Motia prevent AI errors?

The parser is limited to an allow-listed schema, uses confidence thresholds, asks for
clarification, requires confirmation for material preferences, and remains outside the live
navigation control loop. Prompt parsing and deterministic route scoring have separate test
corpora and version controls.

### Where will road-condition data come from?

Potential sources include OpenStreetMap, CCISS/National Access Point catalogues, reusable
DATEX II/operator feeds, municipalities, regions, contracted providers, fleets, field tests,
and moderated community observations. Every source requires licence, provenance, freshness,
coverage, and redistribution review.

### Is an Italian map equivalent to Italian hazard coverage?

No. A nationwide basemap can exist while hazard evidence remains sparse. Motia plans
versioned regions and corridors and should publish the unknown or unassessed share of every
route.

### How are community reports controlled?

Reports do not affect routes immediately. The planned system uses expiry, corroboration,
reporter trust, geometry checks, source comparison, moderation, blocking, audit logs, and
appeals. Reporting occurs while stopped or after a trip.

### How will privacy be protected?

The plan minimises location collection, avoids retaining full trip histories by default,
uses clear consent and retention, supports deletion/export, protects evidence, restricts
provider keys, and requires GDPR assessment and likely DPIA. Store disclosures must include
third-party SDK behaviour.

### Is the software open source? Is the data open?

The application repository uses an open-source strategy. That does not make every dataset,
production service, credential, location history, partner feed, or commercial API open.
OSM-derived data remains subject to ODbL and attribution; licensed and personal data require
separate controls.

## Mobile and automotive

### Why are native apps needed?

Reliable background navigation, audio, permissions, offline/degraded behaviour, and vehicle
surfaces require native phone lifecycle integration. A PWA cannot simply be enabled in
CarPlay or Android Auto.

### Is CarPlay approved?

No. Apple requires a native iOS navigation app, a managed navigation entitlement request and
addendum, approved templates, testing, and App Store review. Approval and timing are
external.

### Is Android Auto approved?

No. It requires a native Android navigation app, Android for Cars declarations and
templates, distraction-limited flows, vehicle testing, and Google Play review. Google's
documented Navigation SDK Android Auto integration is currently Preview, so the dependency
must be isolated and tested.

### Will motorcycle and bicycle modes appear on car dashboards?

They remain first-class phone experiences. CarPlay and Android Auto are car environments
unless an appropriate platform surface is explicitly available and approved.

## Market and business model

### Who is the initial customer?

Initial hypotheses are Milan motorcyclists/scooter riders, surface-sensitive drivers,
cyclists, and selected fleet or municipal design partners. The priority is users with a
repeated, visible route-quality problem—not all navigation users.

### How could Motia earn revenue?

Potential staged revenue includes consumer premium features, paid/co-funded pilots, fleet
subscriptions, and later API or white-label routing intelligence. Grants and financing are
reported separately. Identifiable movement histories should never be sold.

### What traction exists?

The repository demonstrates product work, not verified commercial traction. User counts,
retention, revenue, contracts, letters of intent, waitlist quality, and paid-pilot evidence
must come from dated source records before external use.

### What makes Motia defensible?

Potential defensibility is cumulative: preference contracts, condition evidence,
mode-specific scoring, community and institutional verification, geospatial pipelines,
feedback, integrations, brand, and operating execution. It is not proven today.

## Finance and fundraising

### How much financing is modelled?

Management scenarios model:

- Lean: €2.00m net through 2029;
- Base: €5.60m net through 2029;
- Accelerated: €6.50m net through 2029.

Base is preferred for planning and assumes €1.50m net in Q4 2026 plus €4.10m in Q2 2028.
Dates and amounts are conditional.

### Is €5.60m the crowdfunding target?

No. It is total net Base financing across two modelled events. A future crowdfunding
component needs its own coherent minimum/maximum, transaction cost, valuation, equity, and
authorised-platform review.

### What are the Base operating targets?

By Q4 2029 the model targets 400k MAU, 8% paid conversion, €10 quarterly net B2C ARPPU, and
48 active B2B/B2G contracts recognising €8k per quarter. These are assumptions requiring
validation, not forecasts or traction.

### What runway does the model show?

The Base scenario closes Q4 2029 with approximately €2.017m, equivalent to 12.1 months at Q4
net burn. The result depends on all financing, revenue, hiring, and cost assumptions.

### What equity is offered?

No equity offer is approved. Cumulative dilution guardrails in the model are 18–25% Lean,
22–32% Base, and 25–35% Accelerated. They are sensitivities, not valuation evidence or
transaction terms.

### Why consider equity crowdfunding?

The community-supported mission may create user-investor overlap and public validation.
However, users are not automatically investors, attention is not investment demand, and a
premature or failed campaign can damage the company.

### Is crowdfunding legally ready?

No. A campaign requires a legal issuer, an authorised ESMA-registered provider, counsel,
accounting and tax review, due diligence, compliant marketing and risk disclosure, a key
investment information sheet, and shareholder governance. EU Regulation 2020/1503 excludes
offers above EUR 5 million aggregated over 12 months from its scope.

### What can make the plan stop or narrow?

Failure to preserve route intent, poor retention, unaffordable provider costs, unreliable or
unlicensed data, unmanageable moderation, inability to recruit key roles, absence of paid
demand, or unresolved privacy/platform issues should delay expansion and financing.

## Founder

### Who is the founder?

**Founder verification required before external publication.**

The supplied brief identifies Domenico Campanella Scali as an Italian founder with business,
economics, and management education; experience involving business analysis, financial
services, banking projects, technology and AI-related initiatives; practical software
prototype and AI-enabled workflow experience; and current responsibility for Lastrico and
the Motia vision.

All identity, education, employment, and project claims require documentary verification and
founder approval. The plan does not describe him as a senior AI researcher, senior software
architect, geospatial scientist, serial entrepreneur, or mobility executive.

### Which team gaps matter most?

Senior native-mobile, geospatial/routing, backend/data, product design, privacy/legal,
security, moderation, finance, and fundraising capability. Single-founder dependency is a
material risk.

## Investor risk

### Can Motia guarantee route safety or investor returns?

No. Motia does not guarantee safe routes, accident prevention, national coverage, app-store
or automotive approval, fundraising success, liquidity, valuation, revenue, or return.
Early-stage investors may lose some or all invested capital.
