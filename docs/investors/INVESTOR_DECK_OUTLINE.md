# Motia investor deck outline

**Format:** ten core slides plus appendix  
**Status:** outline only; figures, founder facts, and offering terms require verification

## Slide 1 — Motia

**Routes shaped around you.**

- Motia: planned routing-intelligence company.
- Lastrico by Motia: first working product.
- Status: investor working draft, July 2026.
- One-sentence thesis: natural language can make route preferences expressive while
  deterministic routing preserves legality and control.

Evidence required: legal issuer, founder approval, trademark clearance.

## Slide 2 — The user problem

- Existing navigation is sophisticated, but visible user controls express limited trade-offs.
- A nominal time saving can conflict with surface, vehicle, route-continuity, cost, or
  simplicity preferences.
- Examples: Milan cobblestones; Catania damaged-road evidence; unsuitable north–south
  shortcuts; conditional toll avoidance.

Do not claim incumbents use only time or that Motia has proven a safety benefit.

## Slide 3 — Lastrico: the working wedge

**Current:**

- Experimental open-source Milan PWA.
- Car, motorcycle, and bicycle route modes.
- Public beta routing/search providers.
- Estimated known rough-surface exposure.
- Foreground GPS, manoeuvres, rerouting experiments.
- Report submission with verification states.

**Not current:** production SLA, native apps, national hazards, live traffic/weather,
automotive platforms, promptable routing.

Suggested visual: dated current-beta screenshot with limitations caption.

## Slide 4 — The Motia product thesis

Flow:

`User prompt → bounded preference schema → legal candidate generation → evidence-aware
scoring → confirmed route and explanation`

- User confirms material interpretation.
- Hard legal constraints cannot be overridden.
- Unknown data remains unknown.
- LLM never controls the live routing loop.

## Slide 5 — Why this could become defensible

- Preference ontology and versioned route policies.
- Road-condition evidence and provenance.
- Mode-specific scoring and route-quality feedback.
- Geospatial ingestion and moderation operations.
- Community and open-source contribution.
- Partner integrations and accumulated operational knowledge.

Explicitly state: no proprietary AI moat or national data moat is proven today.

## Slide 6 — Market-entry and business-model hypotheses

Initial users:

- Milan motorcycle/scooter communities;
- surface-sensitive drivers;
- cyclists;
- selected fleet and municipal design partners.

Potential staged revenue:

- B2C premium;
- paid/co-funded pilots;
- fleet subscriptions;
- API and white-label intelligence;
- licensed analytics subject to privacy and data rights.

Evidence needed: bottom-up market sizing, pricing tests, retention, paid pilots.

## Slide 7 — Go-to-market

- Evidence cohort → controlled Milan beta → nearby-region proof → contrasting-city/corridor
  pilots.
- Build in public through route tests, corrections, and founder updates.
- Community contribution with professional accountability.
- Local SEO, YouTube, specialist communities, earned media, and direct partner outreach.
- Paid amplification only after retained-user evidence.

KPIs: first successful route, D30/D90 retention, route acceptance/regret, recent verified
coverage, moderation time, paid pilot conversion, cost per session.

## Slide 8 — Roadmap and technical plan

- Q4 2026: super-beta reliability and claims/data governance.
- 2027: production regional backend and native phone beta.
- 2028: two-platform stability target, bounded prompt alpha, selected new pilots,
  automotive prototypes.
- 2029: conditional Motia Routing Intelligence/Engine/API and wider Italian corridors.

CarPlay needs Apple entitlement and review; Android Auto needs Android for Cars compliance
and review. Neither approval is guaranteed.
