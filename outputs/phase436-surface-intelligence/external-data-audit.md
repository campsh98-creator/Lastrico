The zip also contains a file geodatabase (`GeoDB_e_progetti_vestiti`) and a QGIS project, not inspected.
**CRS (VERIFIED).** Every `.prj` = `RDN2008_UTM_zone_32N`, GRS80, central meridian 9°E, k0 0.9996 (EPSG:7791). `lib/utm.mjs` implements the inverse transverse Mercator; against the server's own EPSG:7791→4326 output for 3,968 vertices the difference is 0.000 m (mean and worst). **Specification (VERIFIED, v2.0 text shipped with the data).** `AC_VEI_FON`/`EL_STR_FON` "Fondo": "tipo di pavimentazione … può avere una ulteriore esplicitazione che riguarda il tipo di materiale", an **extension point not populated in Milan**; domain 01 pavimentato ("flessibile (asfalto) o rigido (calcestruzzo)"), 02 non pavimentato. Precedent: Comune di Pavia added `PAVE`, `CIOTTOLI`, `LASTRONI IN PIETRA` (Phase 4.24, not re-verified). `EL_STR_TY`/`AC_VEI_ZON` codes: 01 tronco carreggiata (0101 ordinario, 0102 rampa, 0103 controviale, 0104 passaggio a livello, 0105 vicolo), 02 area a traffico strutturato (0204 piazza, 0205/0206 incrocio/rotatoria, codes ordered differently in the two classes), 0301 parcheggio, 0307 area di pertinenza, 04 fascia di sosta laterale, 07 banchina.
**Carriageway definition that matters for rails (spec v1.0 p. 71, also in v2.0):** the carriageway area includes "quella parte che, pur occupata dai binari ferroviari, sia transitabile nella sua totalità (i binari devono essere perciò a raso della pavimentazione)". A tram axis inside an AC_VEI polygon therefore means rails flush with a pavement that vehicles use. `EL_TRV_POS` 01 = "in sede propria" (ballast, kerbs, refuges that separate it), 02 = "su sede veicolare — condivide transitabilità della carreggiata veicolare" (VERIFIED in text).
**What the data say about the ring.** Piazzale Lodi is one AC_VEI polygon, zone 0204 "piazza", `FON=01`, with 14 street segments (type 0204): DBT gives the ring neither a per-way surface nor a roundabout/one-way attribute.

**Rails, measured (`dbt-tram-*.json`).** Tram axes intersecting the box: 6,097 m; inside it 3,514 m. Cross-tab inside the box: `POS=02` 2,059 m inside AC_VEI and in no tram polygon (Corso Lodi 1,040 m; Via Comelico 339; Piazza Bruno Buozzi 226; Via Angelo Maj 160; Via Friuli 64), `POS=01` 1,435 m inside SD_FER tranvia polygons and in no AC_VEI polygon (Viale Monte Nero 644; Via Tito Livio 463; Viale Umbria 238), 19 m neither. This is what makes the label inversion in the Comune's `_d` columns certain: the spec reading (02 = shared carriageway) is what the geometry shows. The 18 segments citywide with `EL_TRV_SED=02` are bridges, not shared tracks. Whether "shared carriageway" means cars drive over the rails or a kerbed lane exists is not in the data (UNVERIFIED).
**Matchability with Lastrico (VERIFIED, `dbt-osm-match.json`).** 28,688 m of OSM car-usable ways in the box; **99.3 %** have a DBT street segment within 12 m with direction cosine ≥ 0.58 (Lastrico's own matching tolerance and gate); offset p50 0.5 m, p90 1.6 m, p99 4.0 m; street-name agreement 93.8 % (Corso Lodi 100 % matched / 92.9 % names; ring 100 % / 98.7 %; Via Lazzaro Papi 100 % / 76 %; unnamed `service` ways 90 % matched). Keys: street name, `VIA_ID` (Comune code), `SEGMENTID`/`CLASSREF`; there is no OSM id, so the join is geometric. A tram flag would be attached by overlaying tram axes on compiled segments (tolerance about 5 m; the Corso Lodi tracks sit at median 1.0 m from the OSM centreline).
**Does it add what OSM lacks?** Surface: no. Carriageway decomposition and zone type: yes (parking strips, side roads), not needed by Lastrico today. Rails: yes — the DBT tram axis exists citywide (3,096 segments), while the archive's `railway=tram` ways exist only inside the pilot box and are not compiled (Agent 2 D1/D3/D7). Where both exist (ATM/OSM pilot box) OSM already holds most of the geometry; the DBT-only gain is Corso Lodi (535 m of DBT tram in the archive's pilot overlap has no OSM tram way within 15 m) and everything south and east of the pilot box.

## 5. Licence analysis

(Reading of texts, not legal advice; legal review is advised before publishing a combined database.)
- **Comune di Milano DBT 2020, ATM ds538, ds925: CKAN `cc-by`** (the Comune's `license_list` lists "Creative Commons Attribuzione", Open Definition cc-by; no version in CKAN; portal text said CC BY 4.0 in Phase 4.24, not re-verified because the portal blocks automated reads). CC BY 4.0 legal code (read): adaptation and commercial use allowed; attribution and indication of modifications required; "No downstream restrictions" (you may not impose additional terms on the licensed material); the adapter's own contribution may carry another licence ("Adapter's License"). Required credit: "Comune di Milano, CC BY".
- **ODbL 1.0 (OSM), read:** section 4.4 share-alike applies to a *Derivative Database* that You *Publicly Use*; it must stay under ODbL or a compatible licence. A *Produced Work* (a route, a warning) does not trigger share-alike, and interaction over a network without conveying a copy of the database is not "Conveying". Lastrico's compiled surface tiles are a Derivative Database of OSM.
- **What follows.** (1) In the app and API, showing routes and warnings built from OSM + DBT needs only attribution to both (Produced Works). (2) If Lastrico publishes the compiled database (the repo holds tiles), keep DBT-derived facts as a **separate, labelled layer with its own provenance and licence** (source id, "CC BY, Comune di Milano", date 2020-03/04) instead of mixing them into OSM-derived tags. Whether one merged file can be offered under ODbL while containing CC BY contents is a legal question I cannot settle (UNVERIFIED). (3) **Never write DBT-derived facts into OSM**: the OSM wiki matrix lists CC BY 4.0 as "likely incompatible attribution requirements and other terms; additional waivers required"; the Import guidelines require explicit written permission otherwise. (4) Imagery (KartaView, Panoramax, Mapillary) is CC BY-SA 4.0: viewing and per-image human checks are free of conflict; model-derived labels may be adaptations with share-alike (Phase 4.20 policy: images and weights stay out of the MIT repo; never push model tags to OSM). Mapillary adds API terms (token, no scraping, logo attribution). (5) AGEA orthophotos: not usable (institutional, no commercial use, no third-party transfer). (6) Google: contractual restrictions; do not store or derive datasets.

## 6. Would alternative municipal or imagery data have helped with Piazzale Lodi and Corso Lodi?

**Partly, and only for the rails; not for the ring surface.**
1. **Rails: yes.** The Comune's DBT 2020 would have told Lastrico, offline and for free, that tram axes run inside the carriageway of the Corso Lodi NW arm (`EL_TRV_POS=02`, polygon-confirmed), co-located with 898 m of OSM stone-tagged ways (71 %), that none runs on the ring or on Via Lazzaro Papi, and that tram line 19 shares the carriageway on Via Friuli. OSM could not (no `railway=tram` there), ATM could not (no tram service, no track data), and the app could not use it even where OSM has rails (`embeddedRails` is computed and never read: Agent 2 D7).
2. **Ring ways 111768010 and 111767966: none of the sources could show their surface.** DBT has one polygon and one constant value. No open image covers them (KartaView, Panoramax: view cones and bounding boxes checked). The only ring photo (2016) shows stone on adjacent ways that OSM tags asphalt, which makes the ring's `surface=asphalt` tags **doubtful (STRONGLY SUPPORTED)** and makes the `surface_bike=milanese_paving` key look like the mapper's way of recording stone — but it does not identify the two ways. Candidates that could: Google Street View or Mapillary frames (coverage and token UNVERIFIED), a 5-minute field check with photos (which can then be contributed to Panoramax/KartaView), or the AGEA orthophoto at 20 cm (blocked by licence; texture only).
3. **Sett on Corso Lodi / Via Lazzaro Papi:** OSM already carries it (Agent 2); DBT adds nothing about material. The outbound geometry is unknown, so whether the outbound route used the NW arm remains unverifiable (Agent 2: the 25 m figure suggests it did not).

## 7. What the architecture and routing-experiment agents must take into account

1. **A municipal source cannot fill the surface gap.** `unknown` stays `unknown`; never map DBT `FON=01` to smooth or asphalt.
2. **Rails flag:** derive `tramInCarriageway` from DBT `EL_TRV_POS=02` plus a polygon-containment test (axis inside AC_VEI), not from the Comune's `_d` labels. Rails are not roughness: the data say nothing about protrusion, wear or stone; a rails flag should drive a warning or a low-weight comfort term only after field validation. The Via Friuli alternative of the return trip carries 60 m of shared tram track per DBT, so a naive rails penalty would penalise exactly the shorter alternative.
3. **Conflicting temporal evidence:** DBT 2020 (survey spring 2020) vs ATM 2026: Corso Lodi tracks are in the data but not in service. Treat the flag as "rails mapped here (2020)" with date and source, not as "tram runs here".
4. **Matching is cheap:** 99.3 % of OSM car-way metres match a DBT street segment within 12 m; join geometrically offline (Node reads DBF/SHP; UTM32 inverse in `lib/utm.mjs`); keep provenance per field (source, licence, survey date).
5. **Directions and restrictions** (problems 4, 5) have no external open source: only OSM (relations missing from the citywide extract), the routing provider, or imagery.
6. **Imagery is a verification channel:** one decisive photo per disputed way; open sources are sparse; no automated classification before a Milan labelled sample exists.
7. **Ring tags:** the 2016 photo plus `surface_bike=milanese_paving` on 7 of 12 ring ways (Agent 2) support treating the ring as probable stone; Agent 4 can test sensitivity of the ring with `surface_bike` used as low-confidence evidence (a product decision for the principal).
